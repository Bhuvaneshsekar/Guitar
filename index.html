Types of Guitars

Acoustic Guitar

A hollow-bodied guitar that produces sound naturally through string vibration and resonance, without needing amplification. Popular for folk, country, and singer-songwriter music.

Electric Guitar

Uses pickups to convert string vibrations into electrical signals, which are then amplified. Common in rock, blues, jazz, and metal.

Classical Guitar

Uses nylon strings instead of steel, producing a warmer, softer tone. Widely used in classical, flamenco, and fingerstyle music.

Bass Guitar

A larger, lower-pitched guitar typically with four strings, forming the rhythmic and harmonic foundation of a band.

12-String Guitar

Features six pairs of strings for a fuller, richer, chorus-like sound. Popular in folk-rock and jangly pop music.

Semi-Hollow Guitar

Combines a hollow chamber with a solid center block, blending warm acoustic-like tones with electric versatility.

Beginner Tips

Start Slow

Focus on clean chord transitions before trying to play fast. Accuracy first, speed later.

Practice Daily

Even 15–20 minutes of consistent daily practice beats occasional long sessions.

Learn Basic Chords

Start with G, C, D, Em, and Am — most songs can be played using just these.

Tune Before You Play

Always check your tuning before practicing so your ear gets used to correct pitch.
